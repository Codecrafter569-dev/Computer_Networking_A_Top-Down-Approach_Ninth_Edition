A Deep Analysis of Leonard Kleinrock Interview

Who is Leonard Kleinrock?

Before understanding his answers, it is important to understand who he is.

Leonard Kleinrock is one of the founding fathers of the Internet.

In 1961, when almost nobody imagined computers communicating with each other, he developed the mathematical theory that made computer networking practical.

His research introduced the concepts behind packet switching, which became the foundation of the ARPANET—the direct ancestor of today's Internet.

On October 29, 1969, the very first Internet message was sent from his laboratory at UCLA.

Every email... Every website... Every YouTube video... Every online game... Every cloud server...

...exists today because the networking principles developed by researchers like Kleinrock made global communication possible.

Question 1

What made you decide to specialize in networking?

His Answer (Simplified)

During his PhD at MIT in 1959, nearly every student was studying Claude Shannon's Information Theory.

Instead of following everyone else...

he chose a completely unexplored field.

He asked himself:

> "What problem will become important in the future?"



He realized:

Computers were becoming increasingly powerful.

Eventually...

they would need to communicate.

No one had solved that problem.

So he decided to solve it.


---

Imagine living in 1959.

There is:

No Internet

No Email

No Google

No Wi-Fi

No Smartphones


Most computers filled entire rooms.

Each computer worked alone.

People believed computers were isolated machines.

Kleinrock imagined something nobody else did:

> "Someday every computer will need to talk to every other computer."



That single prediction changed history.

The Lesson

Most researchers improve existing ideas.

Great inventors create entirely new fields.

Kleinrock didn't ask:

> "How can I improve today's computers?"



He asked:

> "What will tomorrow need?"



That is visionary thinking.


---

Packet Switching

The greatest contribution Kleinrock made was the mathematical foundation of packet switching.

To understand its importance, compare it with older telephone systems.


---

Circuit Switching

Traditional telephones worked like this:

Person A
    │
Dedicated Line
    │
Person B

One physical connection remained active throughout the call.

Nobody else could use it.

This wastes resources.


---

Packet Switching

Instead of reserving one complete path...

Data is divided into thousands of tiny packets.

Message

↓

Packet 1
Packet 2
Packet 3
Packet 4
Packet 5

↓

Different routes

↓

Destination

↓

Reassembled

Each packet finds the fastest available path.

If one route fails...

another route is chosen automatically.

This makes networks:

Faster

Cheaper

More reliable

Highly scalable


Today's Internet still works this way.

Question 2

What was your first job?

Kleinrock explains that he came from a modest family.

He worked while studying.

His first job was at an electronics company called Photobell.

His work involved photoelectric sensors.


---

The Interesting Part

Instead of simply doing his assigned work...

he improved the company's technology.

He introduced digital electronics.

Specifically...

he used a device called a bistable multivibrator.

Today we call it a:

> Flip-Flop




---

What is a Flip-Flop?

A flip-flop stores one binary digit.

0

or

1

It remembers its state.

Memory chips... CPUs... Registers...

Everything inside modern computers is built from billions of flip-flops.

Even before helping invent the Internet...

Kleinrock was already helping move industry from analog electronics into digital systems.


---

Lesson

Great engineers don't wait for innovation.

They create it.

Question 3

What were you thinking when sending the first Internet message?

This is one of the greatest stories in computing history.

The team wanted to remotely log into another computer.

The command was:

LOGIN

They typed:

L

Success.

Then:

O

Success.

Then:

G

The remote computer crashed.

So the first Internet message became:

LO


---

Why "LO" Became Legendary

Years later...

Kleinrock joked that

> "Lo!"



means

> "Lo and behold!"



A phrase used throughout history to announce something astonishing.

Although accidental...

it perfectly symbolized the birth of the Internet.

Final Reflection

Leonard Kleinrock's interview is not merely the recollection of an engineer—it is the mindset of a visionary who saw decades into the future. In 1959, he imagined computers communicating when most people viewed them as isolated machines. In 1969, he helped send the first Internet message. Long before cloud computing, smartphones, or ubiquitous connectivity existed, he envisioned computing as a utility available anywhere, anytime. Today, many of his predictions have become reality, and his newer vision—an intelligent, pervasive "Invisible Internet" powered by AI, connected devices, and natural human-computer interaction—is steadily taking shape.

His journey teaches a timeless lesson: history is often changed not by those who refine the present, but by those who can clearly imagine the future and dedicate themselves to building it.
